Mob AI architecture

CurseStone's mob AI is a port of the MCPE 0.8.1 goal system — the minecraftpe/impl/entity/ai decompile (oldminecraftcommunity): a GoalSelector pair per mob, the five controls (Move / Look / Body / Jump / Sensing), the bounded A* PathFinder, and the era goal set registered exactly where the era mob constructors register them. The era code operated on real Mob entities; CurseStone mobs are plain world::MobEntity records driven by MobAIManager, so the port runs through a per-brain Mob facade (entity/ai/Mob.h) that reproduces the era Mob surface on top of the record.

1. What replaced what

The previous CurseStone AI (intent-based: goals wrote a MobIntent snapshot, the manager steered and actuated) is gone. In its place:

Era class (0.8.1 decompile) CurseStone port
entity/ai/Goal.cpp entity/ai/Goal.h (control flags, canUse/canContinueToUse/canInterrupt/start/stop/tick)
entity/ai/GoalSelector.cpp entity/ai/GoalSelector.h/.cpp (two selectors per brain: targets first, then movement)
entity/ai/MoveControl.cpp entity/ai/MoveControl.h/.cpp (wanted position, ±30 deg/tick yaw, speed arm, step jump)
entity/ai/LookControl.cpp entity/ai/LookControl.h/.cpp (look target, ±speed head turn, drift, ±75 navigation cone)
entity/ai/BodyControl.cpp entity/ai/BodyControl.h/.cpp (idle ramp, 10 deg/tick chase, ±75 cone)
entity/ai/JumpControl.cpp entity/ai/JumpControl.h
entity/ai/Sensing.cpp (empty) entity/ai/Sensing.h/.cpp (reconstructed: per-tick can-see raycast cache)
entity/path/{Node,Path,BinaryHeap,PathFinder,PathNavigation}.cpp entity/ai/path/* + entity/ai/PathNavigation.*
util/RandomPos.hpp (body lost) entity/ai/RandomPos.* (reconstructed era behavior)
entity/ai/goals/*.cpp entity/ai/goals/ (grouped: Movement / Target / Combat / Passive / Sun / Door)

Reconstructed bodies (the decompile ships them empty): TargetGoal, HurtByTargetGoal, NearestAttackableTargetGoal (era contract restored from the call sites: they run in the second selector, set the mob's attack target), Sensing::canSee, RandomPos::generateRandomPos.

Not ported (no supporting system exists on this server): BreedGoal and FollowParentGoal (no love/age system). The era pig/cow/sheep/chicken TemptGoal lists keep their item ids (potato/carrot for the pig, wheat for cow/sheep, seeds for the chicken).

2. Per-tick pipeline (era <tt>Mob::newServerAiStep</tt>)

MobBrain::newServerAiStep(world, record, tick) runs the era order:

  1. ++noActionTime, then the era checkDespawn reset (a player within 32 blocks zeroes the counter — without it the stroll goal would fall silent after 100 ticks).
  2. goalSelector2.tick() — the target goals.
  3. goalSelector.tick() — the movement goals (they call navigation->moveTo, lookControl->setLookAt, jumpControl->jump).
  4. navigation.tick() — skips the path ahead, feeds MoveControl::setWantedPosition.
  5. The mob-specific step: the creeper fuse (swellTime 0..30, then the actuation detonates a power-3 Explosion and removes the record).
  6. moveControl.tick(), lookControl.tick(), jumpControl.tick().

The actuation step (MobAIManager::actuateOne) then converts the control state into world movement: the era speed channel × the movement yaw direction (halved in water), the era vertical model (0.42 ground jump, water gravity 0.02 / drag 0.8 / paddle 0.04 from the jump channel, 0.30 shore hop), physics::sweep() against the resident chunks, and the broadcast gate (only changes past the packet thresholds reach the wire). Body yaw rides the record's yaw channel (BodyControl reconciled it), head yaw and pitch ride their own channels.

3. Goal wiring (era registrations)

MobBrain::setupGoals copies the exact addGoal sequences from the era mob constructors:

  • Zombie / ZombieVillager / Husk (Zombie.cpp): Float(0), BreakDoor(1), MeleeAttack(2, 1.0), RandomStroll(6, 1.0), LookAtPlayer(7, 8), RandomLookAround(7); targets HurtBy(1), NearestAttackable(2).
  • Skeleton / Stray (Skeleton.cpp): Float(1), RestrictSun(2), FleeSun(3, 1.0), ArrowAttack(4, 1.0, arrow, 60), RandomStroll(5, 1.0), LookAtPlayer(6, 8), RandomLookAround(6); targets HurtBy(1), NearestAttackable(2).
  • Creeper (Creeper.cpp): Float(1), Swell(2), MeleeAttack(4, 1.25), RandomStroll(5, 1.0), LookAtPlayer(6, 8), RandomLookAround(6); targets NearestAttackable(2), HurtBy(2).
  • Pig / Cow / Mooshroom / Sheep / Chicken: the era passive sequences (Float(0), Panic(1, 1.5), Tempt(3, …), the sheep's EatTileGoal(5), RandomStroll, LookAtPlayer, RandomLookAround) with the era stroll speeds (cow 0.8, the rest 1.0).
  • ZombiePigman (neutral): the era mob ran the old AI; the port gives it the retaliate-only shape (no NearestAttackableTargetGoal).
  • Other grounded kinds (era mobs ran other movement code): the closest family template — hostiles get the zombie combat template, neutrals the pigman template, passives the pig template — documented in MobBrain.cpp.
  • Flight / hopping / aquatic (ghast, blaze, bat, squid, slime): static, the pre-AI behavior, exactly as before.

4. Performance (server adaptations, behavior-preserving)

  • Tiering and budget unchanged: FULL tier (≤4 chunks from a player) runs the full era pipeline every tick; REDUCED tier runs it every 4th tick; chunks with no player are never visited; a wall-clock budget (2 ms base, 8 ms cap) degrades the farthest chunks first.
  • Re-path rate limit: the era re-ran A* on every moveTo request; the port re-paths only when the path is gone or finished, the goal moved more than 1.5 blocks, or the path aged past 40 ticks (PathNavigation::kRepathTicks). The goals still re-request at their era cadence.
  • Node cache without the leak: the era finder cleared its node map without freeing; the port owns nodes through unique_ptr entries cleared per search and keys them with the full 64-bit cell (the era 32-bit hash collided outside ±127).
  • Era quirk kept: GoalSelector::tick stops and re-arms a finished goal in the same pass (era goto LABEL_8).

5. Conventions and deviations

  • Yaw convention: the AI layer uses the protocol-84 entity yaw the server already broadcasts (0 = +Z, 90 = +X; direction = (sin, cos)). The era math (atan2(dz, dx) - 90) is the mirror image; every era rotation is a symmetric clamp, so the port reproduces the motion exactly in the protocol convention (see AiMath.h).
  • Look request hold: the era LookControl consumed its request in one tick; the port holds it for 4 ticks so the goal cadence (FULL tier = every tick, REDUCED = every 4th) cannot snap the head back between two goal ticks.
  • Damage: melee hits deal a single reconstructed value (3.0, the era-normal-difficulty shape; the per-kind attackDamage seeds are lost). Skeleton arrows launch with the era Arrow parameters (velocity 1.6, inaccuracy 12.0, gaussian 0.0075 × inaccuracy) and the era 2.0 arrow damage.
  • RNG: the era goals drew from the shared level random; the port draws from each brain's own Mersenne Twister (seeded per eid), which keeps every decision main-thread-only and reproducible.
  • Mob burning does not exist yet, so FleeSunGoal stays inert until that lands; RestrictSunGoal still steers skeleton paths away from the sky during the day.
  • Doors: the zombie keeps its era BreakDoorGoal registration, and the navigation's canOpenDoors default (off) gates the family — the era zombie shipped the same inert pairing.

6. Testing

tools/mob_ai_check.cpp is the pure-logic check (no World, no network): goal-selector arbitration (flag mutex, era registration order, era same-pass restart), MoveControl (±30 step, speed arm, step jump), BodyControl (10 deg/tick chase, idle ramp onto the head), LookControl (approach, hold, drift), the A* (open walk, wall detour, best-effort unreachable), and the path container.

g++ -std=c++20 -Isrc tools/mob_ai_check.cpp \
src/entity/ai/GoalSelector.cpp src/entity/ai/LookControl.cpp \
src/entity/ai/MoveControl.cpp src/entity/ai/BodyControl.cpp \
src/entity/ai/path/Path.cpp src/entity/ai/path/PathFinder.cpp \
src/utils/Random.cpp -o /tmp/mob_ai_check && /tmp/mob_ai_check

tools/mob_ai_bench.py stays the black-box population benchmark (it spawns sheep through a Lua plugin and counts packets).
